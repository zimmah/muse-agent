1. Breast J. 2021 Aug;27(8):631-637. doi: 10.1111/tbj.14241. Epub 2021 May 20.

HER-2/neu-positive breast cancer neoadjuvant chemotherapy response after 
implementation of 2018 ASCO/CAP focused update.

Wang L(1), Asirvatham JR(2), Ma Y(3), Reisenbichler ES(4), Jorns JM(1).

Author information:
(1)Medical College of Wisconsin, Milwaukee, WI, USA.
(2)University of Florida, Gainsville, FL, USA.
(3)University of Virginia, Charlottesville, VA, USA.
(4)Yale School of Medicine, New Haven, CT, USA.

Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2), a routinely tested breast 
cancer marker, is associated with worse prognosis yet increased sensitivity to 
targeted neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) in breast cancer patients. The presence of 
HER2 in breast carcinoma can be detected with either immunohistochemistry (IHC) 
or in situ hybridization (ISH). In this study, we examine the relationship 
between clinicopathological features, HER2 detection method (IHC vs ISH), and 
prognostic outcomes in NAT-treated HER2-positive breast cancer patients. We 
included 99 HER2-positive patients from three academic institutions following 
2018 HER2 testing updates and conducted a retrospective correlational study. 
Seventy-one (72%) were HER2-positive by IHC and 28 (28%) were positive following 
reflexive ISH. Multivariate analysis showed biomarker status to be significantly 
associated with pathologic complete response (pCR) (p = 0.003), Residual Cancer 
Burden (RCB) (p = 0.007), and tumor size downstaging (p = 0.002) and HER2 
detection method of IHC to be significantly associated with pCR (p = 0.05), RCB 
(p = 0.004), and nodal downstaging (p= 0.03). In conclusion, HER2 detection 
method and biomarker subtype allow for further prognostic stratification of 
HER2-positive patients when 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology 
(ASCO)/College of American Pathologists (CAP) guideline updates are applied.
