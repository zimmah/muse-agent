Early Modulation of Circulating MicroRNAs Levels in HER2-Positive Breast Cancer 
Patients Treated with Trastuzumab-Based Neoadjuvant Therapy.

Circulating microRNA (ct-miRNAs) are able to identify patients with differential 
response to HER2-targeted therapy. However, their dynamics are largely unknown. 
We assessed 752 miRNAs from 52 NeoALTTO patients with plasma pairs prior and two 
weeks after trastuzumab. Increased levels of ct-miR-148a-3p and ct-miR-374a-5p 
were significantly associated with pathological complete response (pCR) (p = 
0.008 and 0.048, respectively). At a threshold ≥ the upper limit of the 95%CI of 
the mean difference, pCR resulted 45% (95%CI 24%-68%), and 44% (95%CI 22%-69%) 
for ct-miR-148a-3p and ct-miR-374a-5p, respectively. Notably, ct-miR-148a-3p 
retained its predictive value (OR 3.42, 95%CI 1.23-9.46, p = 0.018) in bivariate 
analysis along with estrogen receptor status. Combined information from 
ct-miR-148a-3p and ct-miR140-5p, which we previously reported to identify 
trastuzumab-responsive patients, resulted in greater predictive capability over 
each other, with pCR of 54% (95%CI 25%-81%) and 0% (95%CI 0%-31%) in 
ct-miR-148a/ct-miR-140-5p high/present and low/absent, respectively. GO and KEGG 
analyses showed common enriched terms between the targets of these ct-miRNAs, 
including cell metabolism regulation, AMPK and MAPK signaling, and HCC 
progression. In conclusion, early modulated ct-miR-148-3p may inform on the 
functional processes underlying treatment response, integrate the information 
from already available predictive biomarkers, and identify patients likely to 
respond to single agent trastuzumab-based neoadjuvant therapy.
