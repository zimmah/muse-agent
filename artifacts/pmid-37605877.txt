Neratinib as adjuvant therapy in patients with HER2 positive breast cancer: 
expert opinion.

Neratinib is a tyrosine kinase receptor inhibitor used in the extended adjuvant 
therapy of early-stage breast cancer. After adjuvant trastuzumab therapy, 
neratinib reduces the risk of recurrence and, if taken within 1 year from 
trastuzumab, significantly improves the invasive disease-free survival of 
patients with early-stage human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 positive 
(HER2+) breast cancer with no increased risk of long-term toxicity. Diarrhea, 
the most common adverse event associated with neratinib use, deters some 
clinicians from prescribing this drug. However, neratinib-related toxicity is 
predictable, short-lived, mostly limited to the first month of treatment and can 
be managed with dose-escalation and prophylactic strategies. Thus, close 
surveillance and prompt management, relying on supportive care and 
administration schedule modification, allows discontinuation of treatment to be 
avoided.
